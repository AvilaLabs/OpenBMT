# BTCPay Server: practical merchant and operator friction

Research snapshot: 16 November 2026. This supplements the [feature benchmark](merchant-feature-benchmark-2026-11-16.md) and [merchant product plan](merchant-product-plan-2026-11-16.md). It uses firsthand GitHub reports, maintainer responses, and current official documentation. These are selected examples, not a survey or a measurement of how often merchants encounter problems. We did not reproduce the reports or operate a live BTCPay merchant deployment.

The strongest lesson is that accepting a payment, operating the receiver, and completing the merchant's business workflow are separate jobs. BTCPay provides substantial capabilities and deliberately limits some of its POS scope. OpenBMT must demonstrate the whole supported workflow, including interruptions and exceptions, rather than infer convenience from a feature checklist.

## Current requests and recent reports

Issue and PR states below were checked through GitHub's live API. An open issue is not proof that every current installation has the reported behavior. The named versions and deployment contexts matter.

| Practical friction | Evidence and current qualification | OpenBMT implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A restaurant menu needs more than an amount and a list of products | A restaurant operator requested sizes, toppings, extras, allergies, and item notes on 27 November 2023, while praising BTCPay's Bitcoin handling and usability. On 16 November 2026, the maintainer closed the menu-modifier PR without merging it, explaining that advanced POS functionality exceeds the intended simple retail scope. The separate item-notes PR remains open. This is a deliberate product boundary, not just a neglected bug. [Merchant discussion](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/discussions/5515), [closed modifier PR and explanation](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/pull/7784#issuecomment-6906408243), [notes PR](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/pull/7403) | B13–B16: support a useful standalone checkout and preserve merchant-specific sales features through validated integrations. Import the final order, modifiers, and price facts without re-entry. Building a complete restaurant POS is a much larger commitment than adding a payment method. |
| Staff need operational visibility without owner or wallet authority | A 30 July 2025 report on BTCPay 2.1.6 says the Manager role cannot see the dashboard's balances, invoices, and recent activity. The issue remains open. A maintainer's January 2026 explanation identifies mixed tile permissions and node-management links, requiring more than simply exposing the owner dashboard. [Issue 6872](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/issues/6872) | B08–B09: design cashier, manager, bookkeeper, and owner views around individual capabilities. A manager should see the authorized sales information without gaining channel administration or spending power. |
| Reporting across several stores adds administrative work | In an open request dated 1 August 2025, a contributor reports demand seen during conference merchant setups for combined reporting across stores. The existing UI is store-centric; the request also discusses operator privacy and permission implications. This is a project-observed workflow gap, not an independent merchant survey. [Issue 6881](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/issues/6881) | B10, B20–B21: let an authorized owner consolidate their locations' reports while retaining per-store records and authority. Do not make every merchant's sales visible to OpenBMT or a shared operator by default. |
| Local language includes number entry, not just translated paragraphs | An Umbrel operator reported on 16 May 2026 that changing the checkout language did not change the POS keypad's separator and button label in BTCPay 2.3.9. The issue remains open; maintainers acknowledged the need for better support and asked for precise expected formats. [Issue 7365](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/issues/7365) | B13–B14, B17: test currency formatting, decimal/thousands separators, keypad input, translations, and receipts together. Ambiguous amount entry is a payment problem, not merely a cosmetic defect. |
| Updating a self-hosted stack can interrupt the payment interface | A Docker operator reported on 28 October 2026 that an update left the site returning HTTP 502 because its reverse-proxy configuration failed to regenerate. The BTCPay application itself responded directly. The issue remains open, with deployment-specific workarounds in the discussion; the report explicitly does not establish reproducibility across installations. [Issue 7753](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/issues/7753) | B12, B22–B24: the default app should avoid asking merchants to maintain this web stack. Any optional hosted deployment still needs upgrade checks, clear readiness, and recovery. App-first deployment removes some operator tasks; it does not eliminate software updates or payment availability risks. |

## Ongoing operational complexity, with concrete context

| Practical burden | Evidence and qualification | OpenBMT implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hardware sizing, synchronization, and vague readiness messages | An August 2024 operator described sluggish operation and a slow resync on an 8 GB Raspberry Pi 4, with the UI saying the node was starting while it was synchronizing. They subsequently reported a completed resync. Replies described successful low-traffic Pi operation and suggested other hardware or hosting for different workloads. This is a recovered individual incident, not evidence that all Pi installations fail. [Discussion 6139](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/discussions/6139) | B22: show whether the merchant can safely offer each payment method, the actual source of stale observations, and a useful next action. Keep optional full-node operation separate from the easiest supported setup; disclose the verification, privacy, and availability assumptions of the lighter path. |
| Lightning backup and recovery require more than recovery words | Current Docker documentation distinguishes default LND wallet/static-channel backups from a planned migration carrying current channel data. Static-backup recovery requires a separate import and requests peer force-closes; migration requires keeping the source stopped. This is current documented operational complexity, not a claim that BTCPay lacks recovery instructions or a bug unique to BTCPay. [Backup and restore](https://docs.btcpayserver.org/Docker/backup-restore/) | B12, B22: Shop Lightning must provide tested recovery, backup freshness, and understandable exit behavior. Packaging the node inside an app does not remove Lightning state requirements. OpenBMT's current Box backup gate remains unresolved for the proposed simpler deployment. |
| A received payment and an updated online order can diverge | Current WooCommerce troubleshooting describes missing or blocked webhooks and, separately, failures to retrieve and verify a settled invoice after its callback arrives. It provides diagnostics and redelivery steps. This is documented integration failure handling, not evidence that webhooks are generally unreliable or that BTCPay lost the payment. [WooCommerce troubleshooting](https://docs.btcpayserver.org/WooCommerce/#troubleshooting) | B01–B04, B16, B23: distinguish funds received, order updated, and fulfillment acknowledged. Keep durable local events, optional outbound delivery, reconciliation, and replay. A remote store cannot rely on querying a sleeping app; continuous ecommerce availability requires an explicit arrangement. |

## Historical complaints to learn from, not repeat as current defects

| Report | What happened and status | Product lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Refund amount and exchange-rate calculation | A contributor's June 2019 report said calculating a partial refund for a fiat-priced item and issuing the Bitcoin payment took them 10–15 minutes. The request was closed as completed in June 2020. BTCPay now documents a refund claim and merchant approval flow. [Original report](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/issues/890), [current refunds](https://docs.btcpayserver.org/Refund/) | B05, B07: preserve the order's original quote, show the proposed refund amount and rate basis, and keep merchant authorization explicit. Accounting in fiat does not require forced fiat settlement. |
| Refunded sales looked like ordinary settled sales in records | A March 2022 tracker described refunds missing or unclear in invoice views and exports, creating accounting and duplicate-refund concerns. It was closed as completed on 6 May 2024; the original checklist has mixed completion marks, so closure alone does not prove every original symptom shipped exactly as requested. [Issue 3597](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/issues/3597) | B07, B09–B10: display and export partial and full refunds as linked movements; distinguish a refund request from a completed payout and prevent accidental duplicate business actions. |
| Returning only a customer's excess payment | A merchant requested an overpayment-only refund in March 2023. The issue is marked completed, linked to a fix and the 1.10.0 milestone; it is not a current missing-feature claim. [Issue 4812](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/issues/4812) | B06–B07: offer a clear overpayment remedy, show fees before authorization, and retain the original sale and excess separately. |
| Customer-wallet amount entry produced partial payments | An August 2022 support thread describes repeated underpayments and confusion about entering fiat versus the invoice's exact Bitcoin amount. Replies also discuss customer-wallet behavior; the reporter selected a tolerance setting as their answer. It is a mixed wallet/integration/user-understanding case, not a demonstrated BTCPay arithmetic defect. [Discussion 4092](https://github.com/btcpayserver/btcpayserver/discussions/4092) | B05–B06, B32: carry the exact amount through QR/deep links, show the remaining balance clearly, and offer safe recovery. Do not copy a 5% tolerance as a universal fix or silently write off merchant revenue. |

## Assessment for OpenBMT

These reports support six concrete validation requirements in addition to the feature list:

1. A supported merchant can install, pair a wallet, and complete a normal sale without administering a server or entering the sale twice. Optional services and the remaining Lightning backup/availability requirements must be visible.
2. A real order with taxes, sizes, discounts, tips, or modifiers retains the same total and identifying facts through payment, merchant records, and the external sales system. Implement a focused supported integration before claiming broad compatibility.
3. A cashier can distinguish ready, waiting, partially paid, expired, and owner-help-needed states without interpreting node logs. Restarting or reconnecting reconciles the sale and avoids duplicate fulfillment.
4. A refund states what is owed, the chosen Bitcoin amount/rate policy, who approves it, its actual payout status, and its accounting effect. Cold-wallet signing remains explicit where required.
5. Staff see the information needed for their role. Receiving, reporting, refunds, wallet spending, and node administration are distinct permissions.
6. Backups, device loss, updates, stale payment observations, failed callbacks, and unavailable liquidity receive actual recovery drills. A success banner or restored seed alone cannot establish recovery of every payment mode.

These are proposed acceptance criteria, not claims that OpenBMT already meets them. The existing feature benchmark covers many of the right categories; this evidence makes their practical completion more important than adding more feature names.

BTCPay's restaurant reporter also praised its usability and Bitcoin operation, and the historical refund issues show active improvement. Its architecture can suit merchants with a capable operator or hosting arrangement. The opportunity for OpenBMT is to reduce the total work required for each supported merchant workflow while preserving key control. A new app interface alone does not establish that result.
